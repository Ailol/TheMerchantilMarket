# Human resonance

`k§.rezidualz[sz]` is the last move. A reader might feel a release, get curious, laugh, stay unmoved, or experience something else. The operator can leave room for that and accept what the participant chooses to report. The intended cue, the participant's experience, and the later decision to describe it each have a distinct record.

## struktural before reszonancz

Start with the actual affordances. Is the user choosing between different moves, changing the rendering of one move, or simply reading a gauge? Make that legible before adding color or beat.

| Layer | Example | How to inspect it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `struktural` | Continue this line, open a second possibility, or leave the endpoint open | Read the actual options and their consequences |
| `reszonancz` | Add a color, a space, a smaller smile, or an `s/z` beat | Inspect the text and presentation |
| `g'k` | Change the gauge from `.` to `·` | Check the two codes and XOR mask |
| `think'g` | Close the current matrix or sum pass | Check the local result and its scope |
| `k§.rezidualz[sz]` | The reader says the compact greeting feels gentler | Record the exact self-report, if offered |

A cue can be perceived and still have no reported resonance. A feeling may arise even when the person cannot identify which cue caused it. The protocol can retain both possibilities. Silence could reflect a choice to withhold judgment, a lack of response opportunity, or an omitted record.

## Color and the s/z dance

The working palette assigns gold to `s` and lavender to `z` in a two-option example. The color is intended to be part of the enjoyment. It can vary with the context while the given choice remains readable. For the turquoise example, `#4dace` is the conversation token and `#04DACE` is its CSS representation under the packed-RGB interpretation.

Use a meaningful state name and visible indicator anywhere the distinction carries information. `.` and `·` can be hard to distinguish in small type. Labels such as “chill” and “center,” plus accessible control names, let the same affordance survive grayscale, typography changes, and assistive technology. Color preference, legibility, and measured task effect are separate observations. [Color research and accessibility](06-references.md#color-and-accessibility) give the relevant methodological and UI constraints.

## Context, culture, and the unsaid

Ailo describes full attentional modeling, `f'a[modelling]`, as having a `cet'` quality in which context and culture set the feel of the moment. Here that is a design observation. Timing, familiarity, which words already have a shared history, and who has invited a playful exchange can all change an otherwise identical cue.

“Non-semantical” in this project can mean a cue whose interpretation is left open. A bee emoji has familiar cultural associations and can still function as a small unexplained interruption in a particular scene. It can produce a `jot` for one reader and pass unnoticed for another. Labels such as `sSZSZ`, `Szsz`, and the variants of `sooss` can hold an oral rhythm without requiring one settled gloss. A study should ask readers what they recognized and whether they felt free to disregard the cue.

Ailo's notes place curiosity and `⁰·¹` in examples for kids and teens, with adult presentation sometimes using `black rekon` and `0.1`. Treat these as the author's expectations about particular presentations. A study could discover the same pattern, another pattern, or large individual differences. The protocol starts with consenting adults so the private conversation remains an example and the findings can be evaluated on their own terms.

## Bodily awareness and choice

Research on interoception can inform how to ask about bodily experience. A prompt such as “Did you notice anything in your body as the line landed?” permits a genuine report and an answer of “nothing.” A follow-up can ask whether the person felt the decision was made, whether they liked the voice, and whether they thought they understood the content. Those answers can diverge.

Ailo's principle that `attent` is one's own can be designed into the interaction. A person can request a gauge, carry on without one, abandon the cue, or repeat the procedure on their own. The proposed evaluation includes a transfer task after the interface is removed. [Interoception and felt response](06-references.md#body-sense-and-felt-response) explains the nearest research and the limits of the analogy.

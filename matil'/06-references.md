# Scientific references

This bibliography connects the project to established work. The references inform comparisons, operational definitions, and study design. Validation of the `matil'` protocol would require evidence collected with the protocol itself.

## Computation and representation

- Bennett, C. H. (1973). *Logical reversibility of computation*. IBM Journal of Research and Development, 17(6), 525–532. Useful background for distinguishing a reversible local transition from an entire computation that loses information. The gauge proof only needs XOR cancellation, and the path reduction can discard route history. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.176.0525) · [Full text](https://www.cs.princeton.edu/courses/archive/fall06/cos576/papers/bennett73.html)
- Shannon, C. E. (1948). *A mathematical theory of communication*. The Bell System Technical Journal, 27. Provides a formal distinction relevant to this design between properties of a coded signal and questions of its meaning. A short state representation says little about what a reader experiences. [Original text](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/shannon/entropy/entropy.pdf)

## Reasoning and conversation

- Johnson, M. K., Hashtroudi, S., & Lindsay, D. S. (1993). *Source monitoring*. Psychological Bulletin, 114(1), 3–28. Examines judgments about the origins of memories and beliefs. Motivates testing whether people correctly attribute a statement to the transcript or to their own inference. The project uses explicit records as an aid. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.114.1.3)
- Douven, I. *Abduction*. Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, substantive revision 2025. Discusses explanatory inference, competing accounts, and limitations of selecting the best available explanation. Relevant to the third slot in the `kår`. [Entry](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abduction/)
- Clark, H. H., & Brennan, S. E. (1991). *Grounding in communication*. In *Perspectives on Socially Shared Cognition*. Their grounding criterion depends on the current purpose and on evidence that interlocutors understood one another. Relevant to when a conversation has enough shared understanding to close a local question. [Author-hosted chapter](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1990s/Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_%20Brennan%2C%20S.E.%20_Grounding%20in%20communication_%201991.pdf)

## Attention, monitoring, and boundaries

- Nelson, T. O., & Narens, L. (1990). *Metamemory: A theoretical framework and new findings*. Psychology of Learning and Motivation, 26, 125–173. Monitoring and control across levels are useful analogies for observing an open pass and closing the current decision. The framework concerns metamemory. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0079-7421%2808%2960053-5)
- Zacks, J. M., Speer, N. K., Swallow, K. M., Braver, T. S., & Reynolds, J. R. (2007). *Event perception: A mind-brain perspective*. Psychological Bulletin, 133(2), 273–293. Reviews event segmentation and relates a boundary to changes in predictability during ongoing processing. Supports asking whether an experience of closure and a procedural stop can be measured separately. [Open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2852534/)
- Nobre, A. C., & van Ede, F. (2018). *Anticipated moments: Temporal structure in attention*. Nature Reviews Neuroscience, 19, 34–48. Reviews how temporal cues and patterns can guide attention. Gives a reason to control cue timing and rhythm when testing an effect attributed to wording or color. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn.2017.141)

## Body sense and felt response

- Khalsa, S. S., et al. (2018). *Interoception and mental health: A roadmap*. Biological Psychiatry: Cognitive Neuroscience and Neuroimaging, 3(6), 501–513. Reviews interoception and measurement challenges. Relevant to distinguishing a bodily sensation, a report of that sensation, and accuracy in detecting a physiological signal. `sz` in the project can also include experiences without a reported bodily sensation. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29884281/)
- Maia, T. V., & McClelland, J. L. (2004). *A reexamination of the evidence for the somatic marker hypothesis: What participants really know in the Iowa gambling task*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 101(45), 16075–16080. Shows how sensitive questioning changes what can be established about participants' explicit knowledge in a widely cited task. Relevant to testing mechanisms separately from an impression that a choice felt immediate. [Open article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC528759/)
- Lee, J. D., & See, K. A. (2004). *Trust in automation: Designing for appropriate reliance*. Human Factors, 46(1), 50–80. Reviews the role of trust and context in how people use automation. The unaided transfer task probes an ability that a trust rating alone cannot address. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1518/hfes.46.1.50_30392)

## Color and accessibility

- Elliot, A. J., & Maier, M. A. (2014). *Color psychology: Effects of perceiving color on psychological functioning in humans*. Annual Review of Psychology, 65, 95–120. Reviews theories, results, and methodological issues in color research. Supports inspecting the context in which a color is used and testing effects for the selected task and audience. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-psych-010213-115035)
- W3C. *CSS Color Module Level 4*, section 5.2. Defines the accepted 3-, 4-, 6-, and 8-digit CSS hex color forms. A five-digit token needs a stated conversion before it is used as a CSS color. [Specification](https://www.w3.org/TR/css-color-4/#hex-notation)
- W3C. *Understanding Success Criterion 1.4.1: Use of Color*. Explains why color information needs a complementary visible sign when it carries meaning. Relevant to retaining readable gauge names. [Guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/use-of-color.html)

## Study design and measurement

- Stanislaw, H., & Todorov, N. (1999). *Calculation of signal detection theory measures*. Behavior Research Methods, Instruments, & Computers, 31(1), 137–149. Explains how to calculate measures of discrimination and response bias. Relevant to scoring transcript/inference classifications. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.3758/BF03207704)
- Brier, G. W. (1950). *Verification of forecasts expressed in terms of probability*. Monthly Weather Review, 78(1), 1–3. Introduces probability scoring against realized outcomes. Applicable to synthetic cases with a known binary outcome. [Article](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/mwre/78/1/1520-0493_1950_078_0001_vofeit_2_0_co_2.xml)
- Nosek, B. A., Ebersole, C. R., DeHaven, A. C., & Mellor, D. T. (2018). *The preregistration revolution*. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 115(11), 2600–2606. Explains why recording a plan in advance helps readers understand which analysis tested a prior prediction. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1708274114)
- Lakens, D. (2022). *Sample size justification*. Collabra: Psychology, 8(1), 33267. Describes ways to relate the sample size to the inferential goal and the expected information. [Open article](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.33267)
